This is a very brief scientific bio, for more details please take a look at my CV.

Dr. Hamsa Padmanabhan is a physicist who has authored over 60 refereed papers in theoretical cosmology and astrophysics, with over 30 as lead or single author. She has been the recipient and Principal Investigator of the Swiss National Science Foundation Ambizione Grant at the University of Geneva, Switzerland. She was previously a CITA Fellow at the Canadian Institute for Theoretical Astrophysics, Toronto, Canada and a Tomalla post-doctoral fellow at the Institute for Particle Physics and Astrophysics, ETH Zurich, Switzerland. She earned her B.Sc. in Physics (First Rank) and M.Sc. in Physics (Gold Medal, perfect GPA) from the University of Pune, and her Ph.D. in Physics from IUCAA, Pune, in 2015.

Hamsa’s research combines theoretical and data-driven approaches to physics, spanning astrophysics, cosmology and geophysics. She initiated India's first interdisciplinary workshop under the Geophysics theme on "Lessons and Challenges in Geophysical Data Analysis", demonstrating her commitment to cross-domain collaboration. In her current research, she applies physics-based understanding to address challenges in paleoclimate, ecosystems, and environmental change.

She has delivered over 60 invited talks globally, as well as authored an Invited Book Chapter on "Cosmology with HI" in the forthcoming Encyclopaedia of Astrophysics (Elsevier, 2026). She serves as Associate Editor of the European Physical Journal C (EPJC), is a member of the Editorial Advisory Board of the American Physical Society’s Open Science Journal, and co-led the chapter on Cosmic Dawn and Reionization for the White Paper on Fundamental Physics with the Square Kilometre Array (SKA). She has served as the external expert reviewer for several international grants, from the European Research Council, the University of Cambridge, and the Royal Society, UK among others.

Her prestigious distinctions include the CSIR Shyama Prasad Mukherjee Fellowship (2012), awarded to the top four rank holders in India’s National Eligibility Test; the ASSET Gold Medal (2004) for the First Rank nationally; and the KVPY Fellowship (2005), awarded to approximately 100 students nationwide. She was recognized as a Goldman Sachs Global Leader by the Institute of International Education and the Goldman Sachs Foundation in recognition of superior academic achievement and leadership potential. In 2006, she represented India at the Intel International Science and Engineering Fair (ISEF), where she received several awards.

A minor planet in the Solar System—Minor Planet (21575) Padmanabhan—is named in her honor by the MIT Lincoln Laboratory, USA.
