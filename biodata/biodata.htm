This is a very brief scientific bio, for more details please take a look at my CV.

Hamsa Padmanabhan is a physicist, having been the recipient and Principal Investigator of the Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF) Ambizione Grant at the University of Geneva, Switzerland. She was previously a CITA Fellow at the Canadian Institute for Theoretical Astrophysics, Toronto, Canada and a Tomalla post-doctoral fellow at the Institute for Particle Physics and Astrophysics, ETH Zurich, Switzerland. She received her B.Sc. in Physics from Fergusson College, University of Pune, India in 2010, graduating with the First Rank in the University among all disciplines. She obtained a perfect GPA (6.0 out of 6.0) and was the Gold Medalist with the First Rank at M.Sc. Physics (2012) from the University of Pune, India, and received her Ph.D. in Physics from IUCAA, Pune, India in December 2015.

Hamsa’s research spans theoretical and data-intensive modeling of complex systems, combining rigorous physics with advanced statistical and computational methods. She has applied these approaches across fields, from astrophysics to Earth systems, and initiated India's first interdisciplinary workshop under the Geophysics theme on "Lessons and Challenges in Geophysical Data Analysis", demonstrating her commitment to cross-domain collaboration. In her current work, she leverages quantitative modeling and data-driven approaches to address complex challenges in paleoclimate, ecosystems, and environmental change.

She has authored over 60 refereed journal papers in international journals of which over 30 are as lead or single author, as well as an Invited Book Chapter on "Cosmology with HI" in the forthcoming Encyclopaedia of Astrophysics (Elsevier, 2026). She has delivered over 60 invited talks at conferences, seminars, and colloquia across India, Europe, the USA, and Canada. Hamsa serves as Associate Editor of the European Physical Journal C (EPJC), is a member of the Editorial Advisory Board of the American Physical Society’s Open Science Journal, and co-led the chapter on Cosmic Dawn and Reionization for the White Paper on Fundamental Physics with the Square Kilometre Array (SKA), one of the most ambitious astrophysics projects of the coming decades.

Hamsa has received several prestigious awards and fellowships all throughout her career. She was selected as a Goldman Sachs Global Leader by the Institute of International Education (IIE) and the Goldman Sachs Foundation, and is one of the few in the world who has the honour of a minor planet in the solar system being named after her.
